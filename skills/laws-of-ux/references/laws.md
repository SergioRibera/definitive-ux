# The 30 Laws of UX, condensed

Source catalogue: https://lawsofux.com/ by Jon Yablonski. Each entry is a short paraphrase plus the decisions it drives; follow the link for the original text, origins and further reading.

| # | Law | One line | Decisions it drives | Link |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Aesthetic-Usability Effect | Attractive design is perceived as more usable and hides small flaws | Polish buys tolerance, not task success; still run the keyboard and states tests on a pretty screen | https://lawsofux.com/aesthetic-usability-effect/ |
| 2 | Choice Overload | Too many options overwhelm and stall decisions | Recommend a default, trim to differentiators, add search past about 15 items, comparison tables for the rest | https://lawsofux.com/choice-overload/ |
| 3 | Chunking | Information grouped into meaningful units is easier to process | Group fields, split long forms into steps, format numbers and codes in chunks, use headings and short lists | https://lawsofux.com/chunking/ |
| 4 | Cognitive Bias | Systematic errors in judgment shape decisions | Defaults, anchoring and framing work; use them for the user, never against (see ethics) | https://lawsofux.com/cognitive-bias/ |
| 5 | Cognitive Load | Mental effort needed to use an interface; extraneous load is waste | Remove decoration that does not help, keep one task per view, progressive disclosure | https://lawsofux.com/cognitive-load/ |
| 6 | Doherty Threshold | Interaction under about 400 ms keeps people productive and engaged | Feedback within 100 ms, result or indicator under 400 ms, progress and cancel past 10 s, optimistic UI | https://lawsofux.com/doherty-threshold/ |
| 7 | Fitts's Law | Time to hit a target grows with distance and shrinks with size | Big, well-spaced targets; primary action near the attention area and in thumb reach; edges and corners are cheap | https://lawsofux.com/fittss-law/ |
| 8 | Flow | Full immersion when goals are clear and feedback immediate | No interruptions mid-task, clear goal per step, instant feedback, difficulty matched to skill | https://lawsofux.com/flow/ |
| 9 | Goal-Gradient Effect | Motivation rises as the goal gets closer | Show progress, give a head start, shorten the visible remaining steps | https://lawsofux.com/goal-gradient-effect/ |
| 10 | Hick's Law | Decision time grows with the number and complexity of choices | Fewer simultaneous options, highlight a recommended one, break complex choices into steps, never simplify into abstraction | https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law/ |
| 11 | Jakob's Law | People expect your product to work like the ones they already use | Conventional nav, forms, checkout and icons; ease transitions when changing a familiar pattern | https://lawsofux.com/jakobs-law/ |
| 12 | Law of Common Region | Elements inside a shared boundary read as a group | Cards, panels and backgrounds to group when spacing alone is ambiguous | https://lawsofux.com/law-of-common-region/ |
| 13 | Law of Proximity | Near things read as related | Tighter spacing inside groups than between; labels closer to their own control than to the previous one | https://lawsofux.com/law-of-proximity/ |
| 14 | Law of Prägnanz | People read ambiguous shapes as the simplest form | Simple icons and layouts; avoid clever shapes that need decoding | https://lawsofux.com/law-of-pragnanz/ |
| 15 | Law of Similarity | Alike things read as related, even when apart | Same look for same function; static text must not look clickable | https://lawsofux.com/law-of-similarity/ |
| 16 | Law of Uniform Connectedness | Visually connected elements read as more related than unconnected ones | Lines, containers and shared color to tie steps, timelines and segmented controls together | https://lawsofux.com/law-of-uniform-connectedness/ |
| 17 | Mental Model | People act on what they believe a system does | Match labels and behavior to users' vocabulary; displayed value must equal state | https://lawsofux.com/mental-model/ |
| 18 | Miller's Law | Working memory holds a small number of items (about 7±2, in practice closer to 4) | Chunk content; do not make people remember across screens; it is not a menu length rule | https://lawsofux.com/millers-law/ |
| 19 | Occam's Razor | Among equal solutions, the one with the fewest parts wins | Remove elements until removing more hurts the task | https://lawsofux.com/occams-razor/ |
| 20 | Paradox of the Active User | People start using software immediately and skip manuals | Value before tutorials, help in context, skippable onboarding, guided empty states | https://lawsofux.com/paradox-of-the-active-user/ |
| 21 | Pareto Principle | A small share of features carries most of the use | Surface the vital few, put the rest on demand | https://lawsofux.com/pareto-principle/ |
| 22 | Parkinson's Law | Tasks expand to fill the time available | Shorten forms, autofill, smart defaults, time hints | https://lawsofux.com/parkinsons-law/ |
| 23 | Peak-End Rule | Experiences are judged by their most intense moment and their end | Design errors and endings carefully; a strong finish and a recoverable worst moment | https://lawsofux.com/peak-end-rule/ |
| 24 | Postel's Law | Be liberal in what you accept, conservative in what you send | Accept varied input formats, normalize, validate forgivingly; output strictly and predictably | https://lawsofux.com/postels-law/ |
| 25 | Selective Attention | People focus on what matches their goal and ignore the rest | Do not place key content where ads live; use emphasis for the one thing that matters | https://lawsofux.com/selective-attention/ |
| 26 | Serial Position Effect | First and last items in a series are remembered best | Most important items first and last in nav and lists | https://lawsofux.com/serial-position-effect/ |
| 27 | Tesler's Law | Every system has complexity that cannot be removed, only moved | Let the system absorb it (infer, default, compute) instead of the user | https://lawsofux.com/teslers-law/ |
| 28 | Von Restorff Effect | The item that differs is the one noticed and remembered | One emphasized element per view; never by color alone; no emphasis everywhere | https://lawsofux.com/von-restorff-effect/ |
| 29 | Working Memory | Temporary store that holds and manipulates information during a task | Keep context visible (current step, selection, filters); comparisons side by side | https://lawsofux.com/working-memory/ |
| 30 | Zeigarnik Effect | Unfinished tasks stay in mind | Visible incomplete work, resumable drafts, setup checklists, progress on background jobs | https://lawsofux.com/zeigarnik-effect/ |
